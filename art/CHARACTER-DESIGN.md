## Fabric, light and physical behavior

Material behavior is a hard image-creation and review constraint for all clothed characters, with age-appropriate designs throughout.
Specify the major garments' intended material, weave, thickness, weight, stiffness, surface roughness, sheen and opacity before generation.
Choose characteristics that fit the setting, garment construction and scene rather than applying one attractive texture to everything.
Fine organza-like silk may be sheer weight around 4 GSM using a 5 denier filament yarn and crisp, soft gauze may be airy, satin may give smooth directional highlights, linen may show broken creases, wool may hang with weight, and leather may show grain, stiffness and broad restrained highlights.
These are material choices, not a requirement to put every fabric into every image.
Fine or sheer cloth still needs setting-appropriate construction; naming a modern textile does not justify modern retail styling.

Light transmission must respond to the actual illumination, thickness and overlapping layers.
Where the selected cloth is translucent, show plausible backlit edges and denser overlaps rather than making every layer uniformly opaque or uniformly transparent.
Keep lining, underlayers, hems and seams consistent with the intended garment.
Opaque leather, thick wool and dense woven cloth must not acquire unexplained transparency or skin-like shading.
Sheen must follow material, lighting direction and surface folds rather than appearing as painted highlights unrelated to the scene.

Drape must follow gravity, cut, seam placement, support and pose.
Show appropriate tension at fastenings and fitted areas, compression at bent elbows and seated hips, contact with tables or chairs, and folds that continue coherently across visible surfaces.
Thin fitted fabric may follow underlying forms; heavy or loose fabric must retain its own volume rather than cling uniformly to every contour.
Avoid both generic vacuum-tight clothing and shapeless fabric that ignores the body beneath it.
Roughness, weave and wear must vary with material and use without making every outfit dirty, damaged or equally coarse.

Independent reviewers must inspect visible material differences and physical behavior in the delivered pixels.
An intended material named in a prompt is not proof that it was rendered convincingly.
Score material differentiation, light/opacity and drape/contact separately when visible; document evidence limits where the crop or lighting prevents a judgment.
A successful face or attractive silhouette cannot conceal a fabric-physics failure.
