## Required corrections

### Keep her in the chair

The patterned horizontal surface beneath her lap continues into the bed covering at the right.
Pillows and the bed structure sit immediately behind her, and there is no visible chair support, armrest or back separating her seated position from that mattress.
The result reads as sitting on the bed edge.
This is not just the bed appearing somewhere in the background.

In the actual scene she sits in a chair, later draws that chair nearer on one selected branch, and finally returns to her chair after tending the lamp.
There is no intervening move to the bed on `talk`, `letters` or `others`.
Changing the seat also creates an unchosen escalation on paths that deliberately take more time with physical closeness.

Place her on a clearly recognizable wooden chair, with enough seat rail, armrest or back to establish its separate support.
Keep the bed behind her as room scenery rather than the surface directly carrying her lap.
The new speaking angle can be retained.

### Add safe ear headroom

The tall ear has only a few pixels between its tip and the upper image edge.
It lacks the prompt's generous headroom and is immediately vulnerable to a small display crop.
Even if the final tip is technically inside the delivered raster, that does not resolve the framing requirement.
Provide a clear upper margin of at least roughly five percent of the image height without shortening the ears.

### Restore the same sash

Arrival v2 and seated v4 show a wide woven sash with geometric gold patterning and a prominent patterned hanging end.
Dialogue v1 instead presents a largely plain gathered waist wrap tied into a smaller side knot.
The gold botanical gown remains recognizable, but this fastening and surface change reads as a costume alteration within the same evening.
Restore the width, woven geometric pattern and identifiable hanging sash treatment while allowing natural changes in folds with the new seated angle.
There is no need to copy every crease or embroidery leaf, but the visible garment construction should remain the same.
