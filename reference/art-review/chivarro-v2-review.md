## Delivered result

The framing correction works.
The highest horn now has approximately 8% of the image height above it, compared with almost no clearance in v1.
Both horn tips are complete, both hands remain visible, and the wider composition includes the gown's train without making her distant.
Her face retains the attractive adult proportions, curved mouth and pale warm-gray complexion of v1.
The gold hair, dark ridged horns, pointed ears, earrings and choker keep her recognizable as the native lilitu design.

The upper face needs a precise description.
It has raised oval forms under the brow, without painted irises, visible eye openings or eyelashes.
Those forms can resemble closed eyes at small scale, but the actual generic native portrait has closely comparable sculpted forms.
I do not find that v2 has added ordinary human eyes.
It is also not a completely featureless upper face, and should not be described as one.
The softer cheeks and appealing lips make the unusual anatomy attractive without turning her into a generic human woman.

Both hands have plausible finger grouping and wrist direction.
The hand holding the sleeve visibly grips cloth; the other rests naturally beside the hip.
The dress folds follow the waist and standing posture, with an opaque bodice and a restrained slit.
There is no obvious broken limb, duplicated digit or impossible fabric attachment at the inspected resolution.
The fitted dress, bare shoulders and slight smile suit an adult private flirtation, while her upright posture gives her control of the encounter.

The warm plaster room, dark unused dress on the couch, hanging cloth, cup and closed ornament box support the rehearsal setting.
The window looks onto nearby blank masonry with sky visible above it, rather than an open street scene.
The fabrics and warm light fit the project's painted fantasy illustration direction.
The finish is more detailed and polished than the small native reference, so this is a compatible authored illustration rather than a claim of identical native rendering.

## Scene limitation

The sleeve she holds is on the opposite side of her body from the cup.
This supports a woman trying the warm dress and managing its loose fabric, but it does not clearly show the manuscript's precise instant of catching the cuff before it brushes the cup.
Use it as the general warm-dress scene illustration.
If the mapping or caption specifically promises that exact intercepted movement, it needs a pose revision or a less specific caption.
The image also belongs after she chooses and changes into the warm dress, not to the opening robe or severe-dress branch.
