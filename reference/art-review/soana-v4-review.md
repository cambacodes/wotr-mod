## Face and identity

V4's cheeks, mouth and eye area are softer than v3's, but the face remains mature.
The strong nose, brow shape and blue forehead, cheek and chin markings preserve recognition.
The expression has reserve and attention rather than a broad, welcoming smile.
Subtle lines and texture remain around the eyes and mouth.
There is no reason to restore deep weathering merely to recreate an earlier review target.

Her face is slightly less broad than v3's, but the combination of facial structure and sturdy body still reads as a dwarf.
The seated image cannot establish standing height or full limb proportions, so the species assessment is limited to what is visible.
Her silver hair and adult proportions do not suggest a child.
This visual redesign does not reset her canonical age, marriage, motherhood or history with Orso.
The route can retain those facts without inventing a rejuvenation event.

## Basket, hands and framing

The upper hand holds the braided handle naturally.
The lower hand grips reed lengths beside the left attachment, and the pot sits within a cradle that extends underneath it.
The now-visible base makes the object easier to understand than v3's severely clipped lower section.

The right edge is still a real source defect.
Several horizontal wicker bands run directly out of the frame, so the complete carrier is not contained in the image.
This is more than a narrow margin.
The base also has little room below it, although considerably more of its outline is visible than before.
A tighter crop cannot recover the missing right-hand structure.
The prompt's request for a complete vessel and supporting basket with clear right and bottom margins has not been met.

The active repair remains plausible but indistinct.
The fingers, reed ends and existing weave obscure the precise point where a new strip enters the handle or attachment.
I do not see an obviously impossible construction, but I also cannot verify a clearly depicted over-under repair.
For a general portrait this is a small concern; for the carrier scene it reduces the legibility of the selected activity.

## Finish and next correction

The foliage and stone have painted facets, but the fur, hair, pottery and wicker still receive similarly sharp detail.
The repeated angular marks on the pot and skin can look like a common surface treatment rather than brushwork chosen for each form.
The face remains appealing, and this does not justify another facial redesign.

Keep the current face, blue markings, hair and adult dwarf build.
Pull the scene back or extend it to the right and below so the complete carrier has an unmistakable band of surrounding space.
The next source should show the rightmost weave and the supporting base without either touching the frame.
While making that correction, clarify one working reed passing into the left attachment and reduce secondary fur, wicker and background detail into broader painted shapes.
Preserve enough structure to show how the carrier supports the pot.
Do not solve the finish by blurring the whole image or adding a texture overlay.

V4 is a good basis for a close character portrait, subject to actual crop inspection.
The complete activity scene remains unfinished until its right boundary and repair clarity are corrected.
Runtime portrait sizes, small-display readability and installed book presentation remain separate delivery work.
