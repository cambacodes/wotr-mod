# Jerribeth v2 independent source art review

V2 is the preferred native-informed true-form source over v1.
It restores the most recognizable features of the installed Oolioddroo portrait, but the highly polished finish and tight space above the crest still need work.
The expression also conveys less calculating appraisal than the prompt requests.
This is a source revision recommendation, not approval of a finished runtime art set.

I visually compared v2, v1 and the extracted native portrait, read `Jerribeth-v2.prompt.txt`, and checked the correspondence-charm context in `storylines/jerribeth.py`.

## Native identity improvement

The edit corrects the major problems identified in v1.
The dark ornamental forehead plate and long dark hair are gone.
The light gray-lilac face, dark eye surrounds and swept-up teal crest now match the native design's dominant shapes and colors.
The shorter dark lateral points are a more conservative interpretation than v1's sprawling horns.
The reddish tongue or proboscis emerges from the central mouth region and follows a continuous curved shape.
Its full length and hooked pose are authored extrapolations because the native crop does not show the entire appendage.

V2 should be labeled a native-informed illustration of her true form.
It is not a native asset, a verified full-body reconstruction or her elven guise.
The plum gown, fitted chitin-like bodice and richly furnished room are deliberate additions retained from v1.
The prompt acknowledges their authored status, which should remain in the asset record.

## Characterization and anatomy

The result depicts an adult character through the developed body proportions, formal clothing and settled hand gesture.
There is no visible juvenile body treatment.
Large eyes alone are not an age defect here because they are a defining native species trait.

Nevertheless, the oversized rounded catchlights and lifted feather-like cheek edges give the face a slightly cute, curious quality.
The native reference also has large luminous eyes, so shrinking them toward a human face would be the wrong correction.
A more oblique head angle, less symmetrical highlights and a more deliberate eye-area silhouette could convey appraisal while preserving her species.
V1 had a more overtly threatening expression, but its native identity was substantially weaker.
The revision should keep v2's identity rather than restore v1's dark mask and horns to recover danger.

The hand resting across the other is readable.
Long segmented fingers and pointed nails fit the authored nonhuman presentation, and the wrists connect plausibly to their forearms.
Some finger roots disappear behind the overlap, which is ordinary occlusion rather than an obvious missing-digit error.
There is no clear reason to repaint the hands wholesale.

The luminous wings remain a large part of the silhouette despite the prompt asking for a restrained partial display.
Their visible structure is plausible, but the native portrait does not establish the complete arrangement shown here.
The shaped chest and layered gown likewise cannot receive a canon anatomy endorsement from that small reference.

## Finish and composition

V2 keeps the useful warm-room and cool-skin contrast.
The face is larger and easier to read than in v1, and both hands remain inside the frame.
The dark room separates the pale head clearly.

The prompt asked for generous headroom, but the highest crest point approaches the upper image boundary closely.
The head is technically complete in this source, yet there is little room for a portrait crop that needs additional space above it.
Shrinking or shifting a crop cannot recover missing surrounding image area.
Either pull the composition back slightly or extend the room above the head while preserving the existing head proportions.
Do not solve this by cutting the crest, which is one of the strongest native identity markers.

The image has visible texture, especially in the gown, but the overall rendering remains very polished.
The smooth reflective eyes, individual sharp crest strands, repeated metallic leaf edges and jewel-like wing cells receive similar levels of detail.
Selective simplification would bring it closer to a painted RPG portrait.
Use broader material shapes in the costume and wings, quieter secondary edges and less glass-like eye reflections while retaining the face as the focus.
This is not a recommendation to blur the image or remove the cyan eyes' luminosity.

## Story use and next step

The early route explicitly describes Jerribeth waiting with linked hands and later answering in her own form.
This pose fits those moments and makes sense as a chosen image transmitted through the charm.
The background can represent her side of that remote conversation.
It should not imply that she and the Commander are physically sharing a room or that the charm permits touch.
Use a separate asset for the authored elven-guise scene.

Retain v2 as the next revision base.
Prioritize more space above the crest and a less uniformly polished finish, then adjust the appraisal in her gaze without humanizing the head or reducing the native eye proportions.
The likeness improvement is substantial enough that further redesign of the species is unnecessary.
Runtime face crops, larger scene presentation and installed dialogue rendering remain unproduced and unverified in this review.
