## What should be preserved

The face succeeds under the attractive adult true-form brief.
Its smooth eyeless area looks like deliberate lilitu anatomy rather than an injury, blindfold or aged deformation.
The lower face remains appealing, with an expressive mouth carrying the teasing attitude that human eyes would otherwise convey.
Keeping that native feature has not made this version jagged, wrinkled or gaunt.
There is no reason to add human eyes or replace this face merely to address the framing defect.

The hand resting near the second letter and the raised folded paper give the pose an intelligible purpose.
Natural occlusion around the grip is not an anatomy error.
The opaque fitted dress, native-style slashed sleeve edges, collar and earrings retain recognizable styling without turning the scene into an unrelated pin-up.
Warm light on the near cheek, hair and gold ornament agrees with the foreground lamp; the cooler door provides separation behind her horns.
The partly cut-off foreground lamp is ordinary compositional framing, not a second required fix.

The image depicts the general private meeting, not the exact writing of Chivarro's correction.
Its blank papers therefore do not contradict the skull and handwriting described in the manuscript.
The no-brand treatment is appropriate only to the stated post-release history and must not later be assigned indiscriminately to still-branded branches.
The portrait does not claim that brand removal restored eyes.

## Required correction

The upper-left horn apex lies approximately 15-20 pixels below the top edge of the 1,536-pixel-high composition.
It is technically uncut, but that is roughly one percent of the image height and does not supply the prompt's requested generous headroom.
The silhouette feels compressed against the frame, particularly because the opposite horn has substantially more space.

Give the highest horn approximately five to eight percent of the composition height as clear breathing room.
Preserve the existing attractive face, horn shapes and bands, seated posture, visible hands, letter grip and lamp direction.
Do not solve it by trimming either horn, hiding the hands or reducing the character to a distant figure.
This calls for a small composition revision, not a new character design.
