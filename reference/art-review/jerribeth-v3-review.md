# Jerribeth v3 independent art review

V3 is the preferred revision under the current attractive, humanized adult redesign brief.
Its face, expression and remaining demonic traits meet that direction substantially better than v2.

I recommend preserving this face and identity treatment while correcting those two issues.
This is not approval of a delivered runtime portrait set.

## What the revision gets right

The humanized face is the decisive improvement for the user's current request.
The mouth is approachable and proportionate, and the almond-shaped eyes retain their cyan color without the rounded, glassy, childlike curiosity noted in v2.
The lifted outer eye shape and slight asymmetry in the mouth suggest appraisal rather than a generic welcoming smile.
The character reads as an adult without relying on exaggerated aging or exposed skin.

The irregular pale temple plates and fine lines across the forehead keep the face from becoming a smooth human face with accessories.
They should remain.
They are useful identity cues, not blemishes that need automatic removal.
The tall teal sweep still recalls the native crest, although its loose locks are now an intentional hair interpretation.
The wing silhouette reinforces the insectile ancestry that the face no longer depicts literally.

The horns are larger and more emphatic than the small native crop can verify.
Their role here is an authored redesign choice retained from the prior source, not a confirmed reconstruction of native anatomy.
The same distinction applies to the wing count, fitted chitin-like gown and room.
The image should be labeled an authored humanized Jerribeth design rather than a native true-form reconstruction or a discovered canonical human guise.

## Anatomy and composition

The hands are complete within the source and the crossing gesture reads naturally.
Several finger roots are obscured by the overlap, which does not by itself indicate missing anatomy.
The long fingers suit this nonhuman adult design.
A few angular finger joints and the rendering of the nearer thumb merit inspection at final display size, but I see no clear structural defect requiring a hand replacement.

The portrait has a strong separation between cool skin and the warm room.
Her eyes are easy to find, and the dark bodice supports the pale face.
The bright wing cells and gold rim lighting compete with that focus more than necessary.
Reducing their contrast would help the face remain dominant when the portrait is small.

The prompt asks for ample top margin, but the hair's highest flyaway strands sit very close to the image boundary.
This has not been solved by v3.
The horns remain inside the source, with more side clearance than the crest has above it.
A narrow crop that preserves the full horn span will leave the face smaller; a larger face crop will cut the horns.
That tradeoff needs an actual crop preview before acceptance.
The lower wing and gown continue outside the frame, which is appropriate for this three-quarter portrait and is not a missing-full-body defect.

## Focused corrections

1. Pull the composition back or extend the room above the head enough to provide visible breathing space above the highest strand.
Preserve the face size and proportions during any repaint, and preview the intended portrait aspect ratios before committing to the new framing.
2. Simplify selected wing cells and armor edges into broader painted shapes, soften secondary contours and reduce the strongest glossy highlights.
Keep the eyes and expression sharp enough to read.
The goal is selective emphasis, not a blurred image or a texture filter over the existing render.
3. Retain the current mouth, mature eye proportions, temple plates and teal crest while making those corrections.
There is no reason to restore the proboscis or to polish away every unusual facial feature.

## Story and delivery limits

The linked-hand pose fits the opening scene in which she waits after the Commander tests the correspondence frame.
The background can depict her side of that conversation without implying shared physical space or touch through the charm.
The composed expression also suits her skepticism during the new fate-letter experiment.

The current story distinguishes her own insectile form from a deliberately projected elven guise.
V3 can be the approved artistic redesign of her own form under the user's brief, but it should not silently serve as the separate slim elven projection as well.
The crest, horns and wings preserve that distinction visually.
The prose does not need a fictional rejuvenation or transformation event solely to explain an authorized art redesign.

V3 is a stronger base than v2 for the current brief.
The next revision should address painterly finish and headroom without redesigning the face again.
Runtime crop files, small-portrait readability, larger book presentation and installed rendering remain separate work.
