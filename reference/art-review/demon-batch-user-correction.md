## Jerribeth crest study v1: replace the body construction

The visible near shoulder reads as a back-facing shoulder while the bust and long corset front present a different orientation.
The chest projects forward from beneath that shoulder without a convincing intervening ribcage.
The torso then narrows into a long pinched waist before meeting a pelvis whose orientation is obscured by several crossing drapes.
The arm reaching the ledge reinforces the rearward shoulder reading instead of resolving the twist.
The result cannot be justified as a graceful three-quarter pose merely because each isolated limb looks plausible.

Repair by establishing one ribcage volume and shoulder axis, a short believable waist connection and a readable pelvic axis before adding drapes.
Keep the two shoulder sockets attached to that same ribcage.
Do not reuse the v1 body silhouette as a reference for later scenes.
Its attractive face, crest and wings can be separate identity references.

The high dark corset and multiple long hanging layers also dominate the body and obscure the very anatomy that needed checking.
The open neckline alone does not solve that restrictive silhouette.
A shorter asymmetric wrap or lighter separated garment structure can retain her dangerous elegance without another floor-length layered gown.
This is authored costume direction, not a claim that the native initiative thumbnail proves a particular hemline.
