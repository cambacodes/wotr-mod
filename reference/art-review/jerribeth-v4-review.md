## Targeted changes and regression check

V3 nearly touched the top edge with its highest strands.
V4 provides a visible band of room above the entire crest, approximately the requested eight percent of the canvas by visual inspection.
The horns also have usable side clearance.
The figure is slightly smaller and lower without an apparent stretched face, compressed neck or distorted torso.
That trades some face size in the full illustration for substantially better framing flexibility.
A dedicated close portrait will still be needed for small displays.

The wing cells no longer glitter with the same repeated jewel-like highlights.
Their broader lavender and warm gold patches retain translucency while competing less with the face.
The curtains, windows and surrounding room have more visible brush shapes, and the softer secondary edges integrate the character better with the setting.
The gown remains detailed, especially across the bodice, but its reduced metallic glare now makes that detail less distracting.
The image has not simply been blurred.
Her eyes, lips and temple plates remain legible.

The humanized face retains the same mature proportions, slight mouth asymmetry and reserved expression that made v3 successful under the revised brief.
The subtle forehead lines and pale chitin ridges remain.
I see no regression toward a generic smooth human face, an exaggerated insect mouth or a juvenile presentation.
The long fingers retain their crossing pose and plausible occlusion.
No anatomy repair is required by this inspection.

## Identity and story limits

The score reflects a deliberate humanized redesign, not literal native facial anatomy.
The 188 by 244 native reference supports the palette, cyan eyes, crest and angular head details.
It cannot certify the full body, horn proportions, wing count or costume.
Those remain authored choices, as does the furnished room.
The absence of a proboscis is intentional and is not a defect under the current brief.

The pose fits the correspondence scene in which she waits with linked hands after the Commander tests the frame.
The room can represent her side of the connection without implying physical proximity to the Commander.
This image should remain distinct from the route's explicitly projected slim elven guise.
No invented transformation event is required solely to justify the user's authorized art direction.

## Delivery recommendation

Use v4 as the source for the next crop pass.
Another full-image generation is not justified by a visible source blocker in this review.
Prepare the actual required portrait sizes and inspect them at their intended display scale.
A close face crop should retain enough teal crest and temple structure to preserve recognition; wider art should preserve the current headroom and horn silhouette.
Do not squeeze the entire horn span into a tiny portrait if that makes the face unreadable.
Verify those framing choices against the real UI rather than treating this source score as their approval.

The remaining delivery work is crop production, small-size readability review, larger book presentation and installed rendering verification.
