## Game and moment

The outdoor afternoon light, stone yard, old plank lane, side boards, rear fence and wooden pins are a substantial improvement in specificity over the candlelit Together image.
Anevia holds a small wooden ball, Irabeth a larger one, and a smaller spare sits on the counter.
This matches the rules page after Tessa puts down the smaller ball and Anevia turns one between her palms while Irabeth retains hers.
The visible pins are upright and there is no ball rolling down the lane.
The image therefore does not falsely depict either branch's scored result.
Tessa and the Commander can plausibly remain outside the frame; their omission is not a contradictory participant state.
The banners are authored environmental decoration, not proof of an exact native game location.

Pin heads and bodies overlap in the far grouping.
It is consistent with a compact set, but I cannot confidently certify nine individually distinct complete pins from this view.
I also cannot support a claim that the image definitively depicts fewer than nine.
A revision should make the ninepin arrangement legible, without treating this uncertainty as an already-proven wrong count.
The depicted side boards and rear fence give the basic playable lane a coherent shape.
The near transverse timber may be the deck's supporting edge; I cannot establish from this perspective that it is an obstructing raised barrier.
Do not report it as a proven impossible roll.

The red prize is visibly a broad, hanging, edged piece of cloth covering part of the counter, while `start` calls it a narrow strip and jokes about patching a very small catastrophe.
Its presence on the counter is correct for rules, but its size is not.
The rules also explicitly place feet behind a chalk line.
No clear chalk line is legible at the approach in the candidate.
A clean narrow strip and a visible release line would resolve these details without altering the successful character design.
