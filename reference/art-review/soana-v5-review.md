The tradeoff is a smaller face within the source.

A single distant crop should not be used for every presentation size.

The exposed foreground leg and long boot deserve attention before treating this as the definitive dwarf body design.
Perspective can enlarge a nearer limb, and nothing here proves impossible anatomy.
Nevertheless, the long continuous thigh-to-boot shape gives less of the compact dwarf impression requested by the prompt than v4's torso-focused composition.
A more bent knee or a foot placed nearer the seated body would communicate that identity more clearly without shrinking her head or hands.
This is a silhouette concern, not a recommendation to make a petite adult look childish.

The carrier has a plausible support structure and a substantial arched handle.
Both grips remain readable.
The strip being worked disappears among the fingers and existing weave, so the image still suggests repair more clearly than it demonstrates the new join.
For a general character illustration that is acceptable; a scene explicitly showing handle repair would benefit from one visible strip passing through an exposed crossing.

The requested softer secondary treatment improved only modestly.
Fine bright fur edges, individual leaves and repeated wicker detail still receive attention across most of the canvas.
The forest could be grouped into quieter masses, and selected secondary clothing and pot edges could be simplified while keeping face and hands clear.
A broad repaint would be more useful than another texture layer.

Keep v5 as the best full activity source, and keep the current face.
The next useful correction would combine a more compact seated leg arrangement with quieter secondary detail and a clearer repair insertion.
Do not reopen the successful basket framing or add exaggerated facial aging.
