## Clothing and material gate

**This is the unresolved hard gate.**
Anevia again wears the cream shirt, blue patterned scarf and brown fitted leather-looking bodice already prominent in staged Together and her native adventuring vocabulary.
Her bracer is removed, but the outfit still reads as the existing equipment combination with pieces taken away rather than a distinct civilian outfit for a settled home.
The prompt itself asked to retain the scarf, so the generator has substantially followed that part of its brief.
Prompt compliance does not override the broader user requirement for meaningful scene-driven outfit variation.
The scarf need not be categorically banned, but it is not necessary to establish her identity here.

Irabeth's wine cloth waistcoat and unarmored arms are a substantial change from her armored Together reference.
However, both women now wear nearly matching cream collared shirts with similarly rolled sleeves and an outer vest-like layer.
This repeats the near-identical basic-garment issue specifically addressed in `art/CHARACTER-DESIGN.md`.
The prompt's muted green component is not clearly present as a garment, although Irabeth's skin naturally supplies green to the palette.

The rendered sleeves gather plausibly at elbows and cuffs, and the wine waistcoat has a softer, more matte surface than Anevia's brown bodice.
Metal cups, wood, bread and fabric remain visually distinct.
There are no obvious modern zippers, plastic closures or synthetic sheer panels.
Small buttons, woven trim and loose shirt construction are plausible within the game's fantasy clothing vocabulary.
The cream sleeves carry very similar angular light patterns; this is painterly simplification rather than a proven fabric-physics failure, but it reinforces the duplicated-shirt impression.
