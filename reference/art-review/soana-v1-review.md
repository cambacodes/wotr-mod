# Soana v1 independent art review

Decision: retain as a composition and likeness study; revise before approval as final Soana art.
The main issues are insufficient facial age and an overly photographic surface finish.
This is an observable source-image review, not approval of runtime portraits or the complete route.

I visually inspected `art/expansion/originals/Soana-v1.png` and `reference/art-review/soana-native/Soana-unit-linked.png` using the image viewer.
I read their provenance and the water-carrier scene in `storylines/soana_opening.py`.

Its low resolution limits fine anatomical and skin-detail comparisons.

## Likeness and age

The swept-back silver hair with narrow side braids, purple-blue forehead and cheek markings, warm skin and brown-gold eyes make the reference connection clear.
The forehead chevron, vertical nose marking, under-eye bands and descending cheek triangles are preserved closely enough to read as the same design.
The generated face has a more prominent modeled nose and longer facial impression than the small native portrait, whose cheeks and midface read broader and flatter.
That difference is moderate, but should not increase in the revision.

The largest mismatch is apparent age.
Her cheeks are relatively smooth and full, the mouth is softly modeled, and the neck shows little of the aging suggested by her silver hair.
There is some skin texture and under-eye detail, but the face reads as a younger mature woman with grey hair rather than clearly elderly.
The native reference is also simplified and smooth, so it cannot alone establish the missing wrinkles at this resolution.
The story explicitly describes swollen knuckles, wrinkled lips, the care with which she places her feet, and age affecting the effort of carrying water.
The art should preserve that characterization rather than depend on hair color to communicate it.

Keep her recognizable face and attractiveness while adding convincing age around the eyes, mouth, jaw and neck.
Her expression can remain alert, difficult and interested.
Do not replace age with exaggerated frailty, a caricatured scowl or an unrelated new face.
The broad torso, large hands relative to the vessel and sturdy limbs support a dwarf, but the seated pose and long visible foreground leg make height ambiguous.
A revision should keep compact proportions without trying to prove species through distortion.

## Anatomy, materials and activity

Both visible hands have plausible finger arrangements and credible contact with the carrier.
I did not find an obvious extra digit, detached joint or fused hand.
The upper hand grips the arch while the lower hand holds loose material beside its attachment.
The layered sleeves make the wrist transitions busy but still understandable.

The clay pot is readable and its wicker cradle has convincing weight and wear.
The handle is much thicker and more rope-like than the loose reeds on the stone, but a bundled wicker handle is plausible.
The exact threading action near the lower hand is visually ambiguous, and the almost finished cradle does not clearly show a split or newly repaired section.
This works as a general work-in-progress illustration, not precise documentation of every narrated step.
The awl and shallow soaking dish named at the beginning of the scene are not clearly visible.
They need not all be crammed into a portrait, but a scene illustration could use one clear repair cue instead of more incidental texture.

The image does not depict the Commander holding the cradle or their fingers touching.
That is acceptable for a character illustration of the activity; it should not be captioned as the exact guidance or shared-touch moment.
No fox, spirit, husband, returned soul or altered forest outcome is asserted by the image.

## Style and composition

The face sits in a well-lit region against the dark cave, and the eye travels naturally from her expression to the hands and pot.
Warm daylight, mossy stone and muted clothing suit a forest workshop.
The clothing is practical and worn, with no inappropriate youthful glamour or sexualized pose.
The metal ornament and textile patterns add enough visual identity without overwhelming the face.

However, the fur fibers, realistic skin lighting, detailed cloth and soft distant foliage make the result look like a photographed fantasy costume scene.
The composition is suitable for game art, but composition alone does not supply a painterly finish.
Use more deliberately simplified edge groups, broader painted value shapes and less uniformly sharp material detail while preserving the face and hands.
Avoid adding a generic paint filter that merely blurs the same photographic rendering.

The tall source has ample vertical content for a portrait selection.
A face-focused crop can preserve the markings and hair, while a wider upper-body crop can retain the working hands and vessel.
These are alternative uses of the composition, not proof that one crop will show everything well at every runtime size.
Small crops will lose the carrier and should be judged primarily on age, likeness and expression.
No actual cropped outputs or in-game dialogue presentation were inspected.

## Next revision and approval gates

1. Strengthen elderly facial and neck anatomy while retaining native markings, hair arrangement and recognizable proportions.
2. Shift the rendering toward a painterly game illustration, especially fur, skin and background detail.
3. Clarify one part of the handle repair and preserve plausible hand contact; add a tool only if it remains readable.
4. Review the revised original before producing runtime crops, then inspect those crops at their installed dialogue sizes.

The source is a useful first iteration with a strong setting and workable composition.
It is not yet approved as final art, and generated-source availability must remain separate from runtime-crop completion.
