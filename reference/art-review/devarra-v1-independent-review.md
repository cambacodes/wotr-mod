## Identity and authorship

The native thumbnail shows a crimson dragon head with pale horns and projecting neck spines, an angular long jaw and dark wing membrane.
The provenance links this portrait to the inspected Devarra unit records through their portrait asset.
The thumbnail is useful for palette and broad silhouette, but does not establish fine eye color, humanoid face, hair, body proportions or clothing.
I did not render the prefab or independently repeat the archive extraction.

The candidate carries those broad cues into copper-red skin, crimson scale patches, pale curved horns, dark red membranous wings and a scaled tail.
The scaled shoulder, temple and forearm areas keep the design recognizably draconic rather than relying only on horns.
Her narrow gold gaze, lifted chin and restrained mouth give her pride and watchfulness.
She looks capable of judging the visitor, rather than eagerly accommodating one.

Her attractive human face is an intentional redesign.
The auburn hair, gold eyes, oxblood tunic, leather trousers, bronze clasp, letter and tower are authored choices.
Gold eyes may be supported by the manuscript, but this thumbnail does not independently prove them as native appearance.
No native humanoid clothing exists in the inspected evidence, so there is no basis for claiming the tunic reproduces an original outfit or that its coverage corrects one.
Its exposed arms and open collar suit this proposed travel outfit; a later private scene may use another presentation if the story supports it.

The horn sweep and native pale spine cluster are translated rather than copied.
Only one side of the candidate's horn roots is clearly readable through the hair, and the profile differs from the dragon's long head.
A future standing or alternate-angle view should establish a consistent horn layout instead of inventing a new arrangement in each image.
This is a continuity recommendation, not a demand to restore a monstrous face that the user explicitly asked to humanize.

## Visible execution

The face has mature proportions and appealing cheek, jaw and lip shapes.
Warm light models the face without manufacturing deep age lines, while scales and subtle surface texture keep it from looking featureless.
The figure reads clearly as an adult.

Both arms connect plausibly through elbows and wrists.
The lower hand rests naturally beside her thigh, and the raised hand grips the folded letter with distinguishable fingers.
Its thumb is substantially hidden by the paper; that occlusion is not proof of a missing digit.
The torso and seated legs are coherent within the frame.
Wing joints and the tail root are obscured, so their full anatomy cannot be certified from this pose.

The horns have headroom and the letter remains inside the frame.
The stone opening and sunset give depth without obscuring the face.
Fine scale, leather and skin rendering is smoother than the small native thumbnail, but the painting remains visually coherent as fantasy character art.
That comparison cannot establish exact consistency with a fully rendered native model.

## Assignment limits

The current route's dragon foreclaws and scaled jaw cannot be represented literally by this seated woman without a supported transformation.
Do not place the image under a global Devarra key that appears before that transformation or on histories where she remains in dragon form.
The route must establish how she obtains and chooses this appearance, then preserve form state through later pages.
The letter, stone tower and sunset also need a matching scene if the image is used literally.

The painting contains no offspring, partner or violent event.
It therefore does not itself contradict either brood history, but it cannot establish that lost offspring survived or that Devarra has been restored to a durable main-campaign body.
Those facts require the route's actual acquisition and recovery implementation.

Retain v1 as the preferred initial humanoid concept and use it as an identity reference for later variants.
Next review should cover the chosen transformation scene and its exact image assignment, followed by crop and game-display checks.
No image, manuscript, staged file or installed asset was changed by this reviewer.
