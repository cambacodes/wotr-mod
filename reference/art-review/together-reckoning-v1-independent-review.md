## Blocking staging findings

The scene start explicitly seats the wives facing each other, with the Commander taking the third place between them.
By `truth`, the Commander has spoken and remains present to listen.
This is not the earlier moment before the third seat is occupied.

In the image, both women's torsos are predominantly frontal, and both chairs read as placed along the far side of the table, turned toward the viewer.
Irabeth turns her eyes and face toward Anevia, but that head turn does not make their chairs face each other.
The large horizontal table edge, frontal shirt openings and parallel chair positions reinforce a two-person panel facing the near seat.
Their placement on the left and right of the image does not satisfy placement on opposite sides of the table.

The foreground contains a large, unobstructed chair back with open gaps below it and no occupant visible in the space it frames.
The viewpoint is behind that chair, rather than clearly at the eye position of somebody sitting in it.
The result strongly suggests an empty third place.
An offscreen Commander is desirable for player identity flexibility, but an apparently vacant Commander seat contradicts this page.
The prompt specifically requested that the third place stay outside the image; the generated foreground furniture did not follow that instruction.

A lower crop might remove the distracting chair, but it cannot rotate the wives or their seats.
It would therefore be insufficient as the sole correction.
This needs a revised composition, preserving the identities and emotional expressions while seating the women at genuinely opposing table sides.
A viewpoint from the occupied third place can omit the Commander's body and chair altogether.

## What works

Anevia's short asymmetric dark hair, checked navy-and-gold scarf, pale blouse, brown fitted leather and gold-patterned bracers closely retain the native portrait's recognizable vocabulary.
The warm chestnut highlights and softer face are consistent with the established yard redesign and indoor lighting.
She reads as an attractive adult, not a different age group or an unrecognizable replacement.
Her eyes are closed, her mouth quiet and her lowered face conveys rueful recognition rather than sleep or romantic satisfaction.

Irabeth retains olive-green skin, pointed ears, short blue-black hair, visible lower tusks and a strong adult build.
Her raised inner brows, tightened mouth and attention toward Anevia convey restrained hurt.
Her upright shoulders retain habitual military bearing without a suit of armor.
The navy civilian coat and light shirt match the prior yard design; they are an authored civilian outfit, not a claim that the native armored portrait wears this costume.
Neither woman is smiling, touching or staging a reconciled embrace.

The clothing is appropriate to this private disclosure.
Irabeth has no plate, pauldrons or visible weapon.
Anevia retains the native-inspired studded leather vest and bracers, which have an adventurer's leather-equipment appearance rather than ordinary plain household clothing.
That is consistent with the stated identity reference and with the story's armor restriction applying specifically to Irabeth.
If the prompt's separate "NO armor" instruction is intended to ban even Anevia's leather vest, the brief itself should be clarified rather than silently treating this retained native design as civilian fabric.
There is no reason to add exposure or erotic staging to this painful conversation.

The visible hands and forearms have plausible scale and articulation at the inspected size.
Irabeth's fingers overlap naturally; I found no clear extra digit, merged arm or impossible wrist.
Anevia's hands are largely concealed near her lap, so this image does not demonstrate detailed anatomy for them.
Neither character's hands intrude into the other's space.

The closed wooden door, rough stone, plain table and single oil lamp support the private Drezen-room setting.
The warm lamp and cooler background produce clear faces without turning the scene into a celebratory dinner.
There are no yard props, food, wine, captions or visible watermark.
The banners and furniture add a modest garrison context without changing the action.
