## Changes that worked

The age edit is substantive.
Her forehead has natural creases, the eye area has weight, and the mouth, jaw and neck no longer rely on silver hair to imply age.
The expression remains attentive and unsentimental.
She has not become a frail caricature or a different generic old woman.
This now fits the scene's explicit elderly characterization much better.

The original identity markers survived the edit.
The swept-back silver hair, fine side braids, purple-blue face markings and warm coloring remain consistent with v1 and the native portrait.
The cheek and chin markings remain legible through the additional age modeling.
The outfit is still practical and worn rather than transformed into glamorous ceremonial dress.

Broad strokes now describe the cave wall, foliage, fur edging and fabric.
The background reads as painted scenery more readily than v1's lens-like woodland.
The pot and wicker also receive simpler patches of color rather than uniformly photographic detail.
The brighter working strand can be followed from the lower hand toward the handle attachment, improving the sense that she is repairing something.

## Remaining refinements

The face still has a longer midface and more projecting nose than the broad, flatter impression in the native portrait.
The native reference is small, so exact reconstruction is uncertain; the goal should be preserving its main proportions rather than copying individual pixels.
Keep the successful age work while modestly broadening the midface and reducing the elongated impression.
Do not erase wrinkles to approach the smoother low-resolution reference.

The torso is sturdy, but most of the lower image is occupied by a long foreground thigh and boot.
Perspective can explain that length; it nevertheless does little to communicate compact dwarf proportions.
A closer upper-body composition would reduce that ambiguity without exaggerating anatomy.
Alternatively, a revised seated leg arrangement could make the compact build clearer.
A small face crop alone cannot prove stature and should not be scored as though it could.

The brushwork is unevenly distributed.
The stone and fur have strong painted strokes while the eyes, hands and some hair strands retain the sharply modeled look of the first image.
Greater simplification in the hands and secondary hair detail would make the whole illustration feel painted rather than partially repainted over a photographic base.
Preserve readable joints, gaze and facial markings while doing so.

The handle repair is improved but still subtle.
The lower hand holds several converging strands close to the cradle, with the crossing obscured by the grip.
That is plausible and has no obvious extra-finger or fused-joint defect.
For clearer scene illustration, show a single identifiable new reed entering one visible crossing and a slightly paler repaired section.
Do not add more tools and loose material just to make the image busier.

## Source versus runtime delivery

The composition remains viable for a face portrait and a separate wider activity view.
Those are prospective uses; neither was produced or inspected in the game during this review.
Small portraits need a separate check that the older facial anatomy and markings survive reduction.
The carrier scene needs a wider view if the work is meant to remain visible.

Prioritize a bounded likeness and finish revision, retaining v2's age and expression.
Then produce the actual runtime crops and inspect their installed presentation.
The visual progress is clear, but the source image and a completed in-game art set are different deliverables.
