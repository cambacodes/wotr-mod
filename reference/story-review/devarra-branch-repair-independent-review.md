## Characterization and dramatic credibility

The new scene keeps Devarra's self-interest visible.
She treats the spared man's warning as useful without praising mercy as moral growth.
The service branch lets her enjoy Vey's fear and the rising price before she warns the Commander that coercion will encourage betrayal.
On the killed branch, she owns her decision and its lost investigative opportunity instead of apologizing for becoming inconvenient to the plot.
Her final interest in an ordinary evening has more individual texture than an extended explanation of emotional boundaries would have.

The Commander has three consequential approaches to the prisoners and Serevin.
The good choice saves the workers while losing the collector and ledger.
The ruthless choice retains both while the workers suffer additional injury.
The prepared Trickster path can obtain the rescue and capture together, but its failed check forces the rescue and loses the ledger.
These are different results rather than differently worded approvals from Devarra.
Her help with the ruthless option does not become a speech declaring it justified.

The receipt mechanism has a clear required mark, location and acknowledgement.
It uses a prior investigative success and a second one-shot check rather than unrestricted fate alteration.
The weak point is persuading Serevin to step under her own binding beam while she knows an armed Commander and the dragon she wants to capture are confronting her.
The sentence asserting that the challenge is too small to justify retreat supplies the author's conclusion in place of a strong motive.
A concrete apparent profit, a safeguard she believes works, or deception established before the confrontation would make her cooperation more credible.
This is an authored antagonist, so it is not a native-character contradiction, but dangerous adversaries still need reasons to make exploitable mistakes.

The lower-vault lead is a genuine continuation hook, not a finished consequence.
Capture supplies a ledger and a prisoner; escape supplies a worker's directions and a warned destination.
Neither lower-vault version exists yet.
That next scene should test those differences rather than repeat a third nearly identical collector-and-cage confrontation.
