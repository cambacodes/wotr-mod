She asks who controls the road, for how long, and whether its keeper can be bought.
The Commander cannot win her endorsement by renaming an invasion route a crossroads.
He must withhold her name from other negotiations while the proposal remains unproved.
This is a credible limitation on an ambitious Commander without making him foolish or harmless.

`an_unfinished_map/power` likewise refuses to treat declared appetite as a demonstrated price.

`a_chosen_shore/people` preserves that disagreement even when she agrees to provide evidence.

Her willingness to spend time on this invitation follows the completed broker investigation and a signed undertaking already in her possession.
The bridge therefore has an earned reason for her curiosity beyond the Commander's desirability.
It does not require her to become benevolent, abandon leverage, or applaud every player answer.
The risk admission at `the_room_she_makes/prepare` is especially useful: she promises to honor a request to leave while explicitly retaining the capacity to mistreat her guest.
The prose does not mistake one observed act of restraint for a harmless personality.

The boots exchange at `arrival`, the unwelcome success of the exit test at `waking`, and the later joke about imperfect delivery give the relationship a shared experience rather than another abstract declaration.
The Commander's wit has room to fail or receive no compliment in return.
`a_chosen_shore/yes` finally lets him admit that he wants to see her without inventing a respectable business excuse.
This supports mature attraction without mandatory physical intimacy.
The narrow bridge is not intended to supply the eventual route's full romantic or sensual range.

There is still substantial negotiation language across three visits.

That distinction keeps the repetition useful, but the subsequent harbor should act on these agreements rather than hold another general discussion of permission before every scene.
This is a pacing recommendation, not a failing contradiction in the reviewed bridge.
