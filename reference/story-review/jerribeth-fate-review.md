## Writing and characterization

**Bounded writing assessment: 92/100.**
The impossible second sheet, scratch test, failed damage replication and erased answer make the intervention an observable exchange rather than a claim that Trickster can solve everything.
Jerribeth challenges the Commander's explanation and keeps her own purpose throughout the experiment.
Her refusal in `fate_envelope/refused` is terse and consequential without closing unrelated courtship.
The work branch gives her an artistic problem she wants to consider privately; the company branch lets her propose a sharp, companionable evening without manufacturing affection.
Neither branch substitutes a successful roll or an irreversible magical answer for attraction.

The later letter provides different drawings, subjects and player replies for the two chosen questions.
The exit puzzle retains her interest in manipulating experience without retroactively giving her prisoners or claiming that she has reformed.
The bad-performance invitation gives the romantic branch wit and specificity rather than a generic declaration of desire.
Its intimacy is adult and non-graphic, appropriately tentative for an optional contact opportunity.

The final paragraphs explain the distinction between an invitation and a promise more explicitly than the strongest dialogue needs.
That is a minor stylistic reservation, not a continuity defect or a reason to lengthen these scenes.
The four answer flags record a preference but do not yet produce different immediate responses in `fate_letter/answer`.
They should not be advertised as four developed consequence arcs.
