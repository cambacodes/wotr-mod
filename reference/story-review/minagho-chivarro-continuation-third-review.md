## Why the revision now passes

The new forged-orders sequence gives Minagho more than a sympathetic explanation of her past.
She recognizes a genuine atrocity, admires the blackmailer's opportunity, resents his technical errors, and can obtain the correction through evidence, payment or intimidation.
The aftermath distinguishes those methods rather than declaring them equally virtuous.
On the intimidation path she says a true accusation may require the Commander to stop her next time.
On the paid path she admits wanting to retaliate even after obtaining what she came for.
Those are current motives and unresolved tensions, appropriate to the woman whose native history includes the fall of Drezen and deliberate cruelty.

The cult, redemption, dragon, mortal-training and service responses give different reasons for engaging with the same evidence.
Redemption does not retroactively make the order innocent, and training does not remove her impatience or vanity.
The needle, damaged glove and returned book provide an aftermath that is personal without treating a repair as moral absolution.

Chivarro's venture has also become more credible for the native hostess who used lethal rules, social testing and assassination.
She lies about competing offers, nearly doubles Nerath's price, keeps the existing performer fee and puts the purse beside herself when Sivane notices.
Sivane later uses a rival patron to demand a better arrangement.
The profit dispute survives beyond the first scene, and Chivarro chooses a partnership partly because she wants a competent person with whom to boast and argue about expenses.
Her protection of a performance she values is a particular self-interested development, not a general conversion into a patient advocate for everyone she meets.

The room dispute between the women is especially useful.
Chivarro wants a room of her own, Minagho misunderstands and resents it, and the argument does not resolve as soon as the Commander identifies the problem.
The returned book and later invitation let them repair something themselves.
Their mutual attraction exists in the chipped cup, shared fruit, private jokes, kisses and irritation before the Commander selects a group relationship.
The Commander does not purchase one woman's agreement by winning the other.

The adult romantic writing is now specific enough to sustain the separate routes.
Minagho's confident kiss becomes less composed when the Commander returns to her; Chivarro's couch, curtain, robe and forgotten prepared line expose a different kind of composure.
The shared evening uses their practiced rivalry and familiarity rather than assigning two interchangeable private encounters to the same room.
The after-performance night has fatigue, a troublesome pin, wit and a quiet request to stay.
None of these scenes needs graphic anatomy to establish desire.

The revised commitment pages are the decisive improvement over revision two.
Chivarro now talks about another performance, the first address, bad accommodations and letters in the Commander's handwriting.
The open-visits branch develops a half-imagined coastal journey instead of explaining again why a visit is not a shared-home promise.
Minagho wants a place beside her and something worth discussing beyond the Commander's enemies.
The group farewell uses fruit, teasing and a prospective couch, while the choices still clearly distinguish a group relationship, two separate relationships, friendship and open visits.
These scenes now spend more of their length doing the emotional work than describing its rules.

The service path retains a necessary explicit boundary.
It blocks new Minagho intimacy and commitment options while allowing Chivarro to answer for herself.
The farewell does not rename Minagho's unresolved bond or use Chivarro's happiness as proof of consent on Minagho's behalf.
No new scene writes the parent service or romance history to make that difficulty disappear.
