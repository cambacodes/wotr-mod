# Ember and Aivu: full companion arc design brief

Ember and Aivu are official full-content friendship and companion routes, with the same attention to development, choices, artwork, intersections, and independent review as the romance routes.
They are not romantic participants, cameo rewards, or tools for approving the Commander's adult relationships.
This document supplies researched design concepts, not finished scenes or a promise of a future score.
The parent-maintained roster is authoritative for the expanded roster and pending dragon audit; this brief does not reconstruct a numerical roster from memory.

Name-matched source extracts are in `Ember-companion-evidence.txt` and `Aivu-companion-evidence.txt`.
Localization establishes what a line says; blueprint and etude inspection is still required to establish who can say it, when, and under which outcome.
All proposed new scenes and mechanisms below are inventions intended to fit that evidence.

## Ember: someone who does not need a miracle

The invented central conflict is whether the Commander can be her friend without making her prove that her compassion works.
She attracts petitioners, admirers, skeptics, and people who need ordinary help, yet she should retain evenings that belong to herself.
The player must learn her preferences and limits as well as her beliefs.

### First familiarity

Begin during a plausible rest with a small activity she actually wants to finish, such as drawing a bird badly enough that Soot appears offended.
She can invite the Commander to draw, watch, or help find a missing piece of chalk.
The consequence is a specific shared joke or object that returns later, not an abstract kindness point.
Soot's presence is grounded in native dialogue such as `d8ebb5cb-045a-4677-b58d-da8bb2de7984`, which describes the crow teaching her.
Do not make every crow gesture a divine instruction or imply the player can hear private speech they have never heard.
An early disagreement can concern somebody calling her a saint while refusing a hungry visitor food.
Ember should decide what she says; the player can intervene practically, support her, or disagree without the scene forcing a theology lecture.

### Public demands and private fatigue

After the native followers are established, a former cultist asks Ember to endorse a decision about a disliked newcomer.
The invented problem should have concrete stakes: who gets shelter, who supervises stores, and how a person repairs theft without being publicly humiliated.
The Commander may provide resources, insist on evidence, or attempt to make Ember issue an order.
The first two choices can both help in different ways; the last exposes the risk of using her as moral cover.
A later private scene remembers whether the player listened to the affected person or spoke over everyone.
Ember can be cross, bored, or unwilling to discuss the crowd tonight.
Her refusal to perform insight is part of the friendship, not a failed quest.

### The Abyss and its aftermath

When native Abyss preaching and the Nocticula meeting actually occur, add aftermath rather than duplicate either event.
A crowd may have listened, laughed, or left; the companion scene can ask what Ember wants to do next instead of requiring that she feel victorious.
If the meeting never happened, offer a separate conversation about a person she could not reach, without fabricating a demon lord encounter.
The Commander's reply should distinguish uncertainty about a method from mocking her worth.
A friend can help her find food or a quiet place, ask permission to remain, and later remember what she asked for.
Do not transfer her native influence over Nocticula into a romance unlock for the Commander.

### Return and reciprocal care

Give Ember an opportunity to notice the Commander's weariness and suggest something ordinary rather than dispense a perfect moral diagnosis.
She can also be wrong about what they need, listen to correction, and adapt.
The player should be able to admit fear, ask for distraction, or prefer company without speaking.
A later gathering with the Brotherhood tests whether the Commander respects the difference between Ember's wish and the group's enthusiasm.
The payoff is a life in which she can help people without being available to everyone at every moment.

### Native outcome continuity

For the compassionate native outcome, continue her aid and preaching while protecting her freedom to decline leadership.
For the firmer native outcome, inspect its exact predicates before authoring; allow her concern about frightening people to remain distinct from delight in punishment.
Native line `8b6a9f20-ed67-45e3-b9f6-0baa31755b48` explicitly describes distress at using a frightening trick to avoid killing.
For the devastated outcome, replace debate-driven scenes with familiar routines, low-pressure choices, and reliable companionship.
Do not reset her condition, summon confident speeches, or claim that one joke repairs profound harm.
A recovery development may be possible as separately researched and authored content, but friendship itself is valuable without a cure.
For an ignored or departed Ember, acknowledge absence rather than award the same intimate ending.
Native departure reference `20076373-d68d-47f2-84c5-e490eab31422` supports continued independent wayfaring.

### Trickster access and endings

An invented Trickster opening can recover a lost invitation or reveal a road to where a missed companion now lives.
If she was never recruited, begin with actual recognition and a new meeting, not memories of shared campaigns.
If she is retained but dead, require a verified recovery action before the living encounter.
If the Commander harmed her, recovery does not erase that history; she may accept practical help while withholding renewed trust.
The native companion and her existing quest progression remain authoritative, with the friendship layered alongside them.
Endings should distinguish continuing travel together, warm correspondence during her independent work, dependable care after devastation, distance after unresolved harm, and genuine bereavement if recovery did not happen.
Ascended continuation must respect her native difficulty learning the limits of divine help, described at `5054c4f7-bc07-46e8-945d-924757bd0d26`.
None should declare that the Commander owns her moral purpose.

## Aivu: brave enough to come back, free enough to go home

The invented central conflict is the difference between being valued as a friend and being valued as a powerful dragon who makes everyone happy.
Her arc should contain exuberance, mistakes, fear, responsibility, and discoveries she initiates herself.
Cookies remain a recognizable pleasure, not the answer to every emotional beat.

### Arrival and a project of her own

On Azata saves, use the existing Aivu and her established bond.
On a first Trickster meeting, do not pretend an Azata introduction has occurred.
Give her a project such as mapping the best places to see Drezen from above, with exaggerated names and one genuinely useful observation.
She wants the Commander to take it seriously but may need help noticing a frightened bystander or a damaged roof.
The player can join the game, help her repair the mistake, or encourage spectacle at someone else's expense.
The later consequence should be whether the affected person trusts her, not a generic obedience reward.

### Fright and courage

After the native kidnapping, use its actual outcome before referencing the rescue.
Aivu can choose a route that avoids a cramped passage, insist she is fine, and later admit why she dislikes it.
The Commander can accompany her, accept another route, or pressure her to prove bravery.
She should subsequently remember pressure or patience and have a chance to confront the Commander.
On saves without the kidnapping, use a different incident, such as the first time someone panics at her wings, and do not borrow the captivity confession.
A later act of courage should be chosen by her and useful to somebody else, not a repeat kidnapping staged to make the player indispensable.

### More than morale

An invented community scene has two frightened groups ask Aivu for incompatible kinds of reassurance.
She begins by promising too much and must choose whom to ask for help.
The Commander can support delegation or insist she keep entertaining everyone herself.
Let her discover that an apology and a smaller promise can be more useful than a grand performance.
This develops her native disgust at slavery and willingness to resist it without turning her into an adult policy adviser.
When other dragons are available, she can ask a practical question that adults complicate unnecessarily, then discover one thing their caution gets right.

### Home and changing power

Give Elysium and her family emotional weight beyond a place she disappears to when the route ends.
She can miss a particular family habit while enjoying the Commander's company.
The player may help plan a visit, admit they will miss her, or try to make leaving feel like betrayal.
She should reject the last framing in her own direct voice.
Native departure caused by lost power needs an actual safe continuation mechanism before any future visit is staged in a dangerous location.
Remaining friends at a distance is a full outcome, not failure to retain a pet.

### Trickster access and endings

Invented Trickster mechanism: a deliberately misplaced invitation reaches Elysium and opens a limited, safe meeting place that Aivu can enter and leave voluntarily.
This is a new meeting route, not proof that she has already accompanied a non-Azata Commander through the Abyss.
Keep safe social visits distinct from combat recruitment and mythic scaling; any playable-companion extension requires its own engine work and verification.
If a native Aivu already exists, reuse that identity rather than generating a second dragon.
If she departed because of cruelty, start with her actual objection and allow continued refusal.
If she was banished, locate her before proposing a return; if killed or devoured, an actual restoration mechanism and aftermath are separate requirements.
Other mythics can have friendship where the character and circumstances support it, without a universal demand that she remain beside an actively cruel Commander.
Endings should cover continued adventures, visits from Elysium, a warm farewell followed by later reunion, distant friendship after lost power, unresolved estrangement, and loss.
Her independent eventual adventures have native support at `d7c23050-87cb-4182-8e25-93fbcaf11901`.
Do not age her up in campaign artwork or use an adult future to change the route's nonromantic nature.

## Shared scenes and wider intersections

Ember and Aivu already share games, so their new paired scenes should sometimes begin without the Commander.
An invented scavenger hunt can reveal that Ember prefers noticing overlooked things while Aivu wants spectacular discoveries.
A dispute over a prize gives them something to resolve between themselves instead of asking the Commander to choose the better child.
A later shared scene can have Aivu mistake Ember's quiet for sadness; Ember may simply want to finish a drawing alone.
This teaches the dragon something particular about her friend, not a general consent lecture.
After an actual kidnapping, Ember can offer company and Aivu can ask for a game rather than another discussion of fear.
Neither becomes the other's therapist.
A final joint project, such as illustrated directions to a safe public meal, lets both contribute and changes according to earlier choices about hospitality and repair.
The friendship must still progress independently if one companion is unavailable.

Kiana and Aranka can host a rehearsal with meaningful parts for both, including an option to decline performing.
Arsinoe can help with practical shelter or food while Ember disputes whose needs receive attention first.
Seelah can share an ordinary outing in which protective concern meets the companions' own plans.
Wenduag requires outcome-sensitive treatment: native `5442dcf4-e1dd-47f5-b41a-c127471418f0` can have her destroy Ember's orphanage, so shared warmth cannot override that history.
Aivu's native visits to Arueshalae, `81bbf75f-bcc2-4dac-a9c0-ad05d259d8ee`, support a friendly food-and-garden intersection without implying romance.
Targona, Terendelev, and other audited dragons can enter only when actually available; Aivu's questions should not certify an adult character as redeemed or romance-ready.
Adult romantic scenes and friendship scenes should have separate presentation contexts, even when they share locations or supporting characters.

## Art direction and verification

Ember's visual motifs should include her worn clothing, burned hands, Soot, chalk, rain shelter, and everyday acts of help, checked against the installed portrait and game model.
Use expression and activity to distinguish curiosity, annoyance, tiredness, delight, and distress.
Do not beautify away scars, add missing fingers, age her into an adult romance design, or depict every scene as a glowing religious icon.
Aivu's motifs should include her native colors and iridescent wings, expressive paws and tail, an overambitious map, flight, family reminders, and quieter moments after fear.
Use her actual progression stage for scale; a safe small-dragon visit cannot reuse artwork implying a much larger combat body.
Shared art should show their independent attention and actions rather than two figures waiting for the Commander to approve them.
Images need correct anatomy, readable silhouettes and expressions at portrait size, scene-appropriate props, and consistency with actual branch staging.
Art review should judge faithful and expressive companionship, with no romance-intensity criterion.

Headless review should traverse starts, late starts, native quest outcomes, lost contact, recovery, conflicting flags, optional intersections, and endings.
It should verify that these routes use friendship progress rather than lover or commitment flags and never depend on active romance counts.
Existing Ember quest variants, the native Azata Aivu entity, mythic transitions, rest events, save reloads, and scene completion must remain coherent.
A native kidnapping cannot be claimed without its history, and a projected or visiting Aivu cannot silently become a combat pet.
Restoration must change the required game state before living scenes unlock.
Paired scenes need both actual participants while solo progression survives the other's absence.
Rendered review must inspect portrait crops, subtitles, speaker identity, readable choices, and age-appropriate presentation in the real UI.
Writing reviewers should assess voice, emotional depth, reciprocity, meaningful choices, continuity, and pacing with the same rigor used for the romance arcs.
No scene count, image count, passing score, or release readiness is promised before the corresponding work exists and has been inspected.
