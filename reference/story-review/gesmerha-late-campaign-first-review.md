## Dramatic and character assessment

The inheritance dispute gives Gesmerha a personal interest that can conflict with her role as a teacher or chief.
Sella wants her grandmother's property rather than the opportunity to deliver a lesson about generosity.
Gesmerha acknowledges that she wants the patterns and may dislike the answer she accepts.
Those competing motives sustain the disagreement without requiring an obviously malicious opponent.

The copy and loan choices have different later costs.
Copies delay Gesmerha's tools and are distributed among teachers; a loan preserves Sella's control and leaves the apprentices teaching from available knowledge.
The Nature check determines whether the delivery reaches a lesson on time.
Failure and the nonroll detour preserve the box but lead to a lesson held without Gesmerha, with a subsequent conversation about the work proceeding in her absence.
These are narrated costs, not implemented inventory, gold or injury changes.

The private evening develops attraction through reciprocal touch, a troublesome fastener, laughter and a cramped bed.
Its intimacy remains non-graphic, with separate choices for closeness and pace.
The friendship evening has jokes and pleasure unrelated to repairing the clan.
Gesmerha's blindness remains consequential through described positions, touch and sound, without a cure or supernatural visual knowledge.
The earlier campaign's paid Dera afternoons establish the previously delayed tools referred to by this contribution.

Some explanations of personal boundaries repeat themes already expressed by the choices themselves.
The specific ending corrections above address the clearest narrator intrusions; the final reread should still prefer particular memories over generic relationship assurances.
