## Character and relationship judgment

The strongest dramatic question is whether Targona can describe the protection she gave other prisoners without making a justification for her captivity.
That grows from the native Protectress account rather than inventing a generic wounded angel.
Native dialogue also gives her conflicting joy and distress after freedom and a concern about spiritual corruption that she distinguishes from physical appearance.
The extension does not reduce those concerns to looking unattractive or have affection cure her altered body.

Publishing and withholding the account both have consequences.
Publication produces an editorial argument and incompatible reader reactions.
Privacy preserves her pages but leaves a public account she finds painfully small.
Neither branch awards complete control over how others describe her.
Her own crossed-out sentence, anger, pleasure in opening a paper door, and temptation to ask for a larger mythic intervention make the internal conflict specific.

Anograt's voice has a sharper comic edge, especially the lock's deserved retirement and the violent interpretation of an uplifting account.
The revised shared-table complaint is more natural than reciting her separation constraints.
It remains compatible with those constraints and does not stage independent travel.
The installed parent already establishes her nickname for Targona, separate opinions, temporary separation, and shared affection.
The new letters continue those facts rather than claiming to originate a triad.

The romantic conclusion is deliberately restrained but carries recognizable desire in Targona missing kisses and wanting time without a page between them.
It fits an established relationship and does not make disclosure a price for intimacy.
The friendship conclusion asks for ordinary news without trying to convert trust into romance.
No graphic sexual content appears.

Some interpretive language remains polished and self-conscious, particularly the repeated discussion of stories, usefulness, and accounts.
It now suits a focused correspondence arc, but a further six scenes on the same motif would narrow the character too much.
Future development should let her act as a celestial warrior, disagree over a present problem, and enjoy a shared activity beyond another writing exercise.
That is an assembled-route need, not a demand to manufacture physical presence in this contribution.
