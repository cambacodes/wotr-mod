## All-path availability and fate revision

Availability on every mythic path is compatible with different scenes, relationship forms, and consequences.
It does not justify having all women deliver the same approval after every action.
Early mythic selection, completed transformation, character survival, and companion departure require separate facts.
For Lich, author how affection continues after bodily transformation and what each woman refuses to excuse.
For Swarm, a normal dinner scene with an ordinary bodily Commander is inconsistent unless the mod has established an actual physical alternative.
A severed companion relationship needs a credible reunion, not a removed condition alone.
For Aeon timeline changes, define what memory survives and which version of each person is present.
For Legend and Gold Dragon, preserve previously established relationship history across the path transition.
Angel, Azata, Demon, Devil, and Trickster need differing responses where power, loyalty, or harm changes the meaning of the scene.
Do not add blanket path bans.
Do not count inaccessible characters as supported because a scene exists in JSON.

The user's initial Trickster campaign permits a deliberately authored fate-changing branch.
The most defensible version reopens an opportunity, restores a person, or creates a meeting that a prior outcome closed.
The restored woman retains her motives and makes her own decision about the Commander.
Her changed circumstances cannot serve as retroactive consent.
A joke that tears up a death notice can be a strong scene only if its claimed consequences become true in the game.
A dream, illusion, or remembered encounter is a separate kind of content and does not satisfy a promise of actual rescue or an available living partner.

A real fate revision needs evidence that the target's presence, original quest consumers, relevant death and departure state, later dialogue, save/reload state, and epilogue agree.
Restoring Kiana cannot leave the world simultaneously treating her soul as trapped.
Restoring Jerribeth cannot leave all subsequent checks treating her as dead.
Restoring Chivarro must account for both her fate and the Minagho relationship already in R&R.
Clearing one death flag is insufficient evidence because unit death and quest state can persist independently.
Changes to the past must be stated as authored departures from canon, rather than presented as something the base game always implied.

Wenduag can propose an introduction, assess another woman's ambition, or tempt the Commander toward a risky arrangement.
She cannot obtain a person as romantic property.
An adult consensual power dynamic can fit the project only when the woman chooses it freely and can stop.
Her personal development stage matters: early dominance games and later earned trust are not interchangeable voices.

## Canon rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence required |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Character motives and voice | 30 | Scene-by-scene comparison with attributed source dialogue and established convictions. |
| Quest and fate continuity | 25 | Conditions and outcomes match survival, rescue, departure, chapter, and prior relationship facts. |
| Mythic and transformation coherence | 15 | Authored differences where the path changes bodies, memory, ethics, or availability. |
| Relationship history and agency | 15 | Existing partners are acknowledged, attraction is developed, and consent is independent of coercion or rescue debt. |
| Visual identity | 10 | Original and generated portraits compared for face, species, age presentation, build, and distinctive traits. |
| Existing R&R continuity | 5 | Route flags, introductions, and endings extend installed content without competing versions. |

A release needs at least 90/100 on this rubric and no major unresolved inconsistency.
A severe error blocks release even if other criteria would lift the arithmetic score above 90.
Examples include a dead person treated as alive without restoration, Seelah casually approving atrocities, a generic succubus replacing Jerribeth, or a second Minagho throuple contradicting R&R.
Every deduction must identify a scene or artifact and explain the inconsistency.
Unwritten scenes, unseen images, and unexecuted fate rewrites remain unscored.
A reviewer score is an editorial judgment, not a probability that the game will work.
Headless tests can check conditions and state transitions, but cannot prove recognizable characterization by counting keywords.
