# Independent story review

Reviewer stance: a candid developmental editor who values subtext, warmth, and consequential conflict.
Review date: 2026-09-25.
This is a baseline review and an expansion brief, not approval of unwritten routes.
No new route has earned a score.

## Evidence and limits

I read the existing authoring source in `story.py`, its scene model and eligibility in `src/Story.cs`, and targeted English dialogue from the installed game.
Canon excerpts with localization keys are preserved under `reference/story-review/` for Konomi, Jerribeth, Kiana, Vellexia, Seelah, Arsinoe, Gesmerha, Chivarro, Minagho, and Wenduag.
These extracts match character names in localized strings and include other speakers discussing them.
They establish facts and useful voice samples, but do not by themselves identify every speaker, quest condition, or blueprint hook.
Runtime integration needs the corresponding blueprints.
The installed RanRomance `LocalizedStrings.json` already includes a Minagho and Chivarro throuple ending.
Any expansion must first inspect that route's state and avoid offering an independent duplicate marriage.

## Existing Tirabade baseline

The opening scenes have convincing individual voices.
Anevia's joke about bread that could stop an arrow and Irabeth's admission that she has answers for several kinds of emergency turn occupations into humor without making either woman an occupation.
The scabbard knocking Anevia's knees is especially good marital detail.
Keep it.
The cold cup, stolen pen, and damaged glove place affection inside daily work.
These objects earn later callbacks because they first serve a scene.

The affair scenes acknowledge that attraction and loyalty can coexist painfully.
Irabeth's insistence that marrying Anevia was her best choice protects the existing marriage from becoming a convenient mistake.
Anevia's reaction to discovering the other affair has a sharp and credible contradiction: her first hurt is that Irabeth did not tell her.
The writer allows this hypocrisy to hurt before explaining it.
More of that unevenness would help the later route.

The back half becomes too fluent at relationship analysis.
`table`, `ordinary`, `a_self`, `power`, and `future` repeatedly arrive at the same lesson through differently arranged discussions about privacy, promises, independent lives, and power.
The arguments are sensible, but almost everyone understands the exact emotional problem and articulates it in finished prose.
Irabeth and Anevia should occasionally misunderstand each other for more than one node.
One partner can fix a practical problem while still misreading the hurt that caused it.
Let the player experience the result in a later meeting.

The prose often explains the moral meaning after an action has already shown it.
For example, the plate-stealing exchange in `ordinary` already demonstrates anger giving way to companionship.
The final explanation that laughter does not erase the quarrel repeats the point.
Cut that kind of interpretive final sentence where the gesture stands on its own.
The physical vocabulary is also narrow: hands, glances, chairs, lowering voices, and quiet smiles carry many emotional turns.
A token count is not a quality test, but the repeated room-and-table setting deserves attention when adding eight more romances.

The current structure makes too many player choices a choice between the emotionally literate answer and an obviously cruel answer.
Add two defensible desires that cannot both be satisfied tonight.
Anevia wants to keep a promise to a friend while Irabeth wants the rare uninterrupted evening already arranged.
The Commander wants to accompany one woman on a risky errand while the other believes that turns private affection into visible favoritism.
A worthwhile branch lets someone be disappointed without making the player a villain or immediately closing the romance.

The existing private nights are graphic and explicit and affectionate.
They are differentiated in speech, but both follow a similar pause, kiss, darkness, and reflective aftermath sequence.
New scenes should vary who initiates, who makes the other laugh, what interruption occurs, and what small trace remains the next morning.
Romantic tension can come from a wager, choosing a partner for a dance, an almost-private remark at a public event, or a reunion that starts with an argument.
It does not need anatomical detail.

## Rubric for completed playable routes

Each reviewer scores the same delivered revision independently and cites scene/node IDs for deductions.
The editorial target is at least 90/100 per reviewer, not an average that hides a failing review.
An unavailable image, unwritten branch, or unexecuted check is marked unreviewed rather than awarded assumed points.
An automated repetition scan is supporting evidence, never an art or writing judge.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence required |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Character voice and canon fidelity | 30 | Distinct speech, retained convictions and relationships, canon references, appearance and species consistency |
| Adult emotional complexity | 20 | Conflicting wants, earned attachment, believable reactions to affairs and power, women retaining goals outside romance |
| Romantic tension | 15 | Specific chemistry, initiative from both sides, anticipation and graphic and explicit intimacy that changes with the relationship |
| Branching and consequences | 20 | At least two substantially different outcomes, remembered choices, meaningful links, credible all-path continuation |
| Prose and pacing | 15 | Varied scene activity, economical narration, differentiated exchanges, enough ordinary pleasure between crises |

A score cannot compensate for a dead or absent character appearing without explanation, a second mod resetting an established romance, or a coercion effect being treated as voluntary affection.
Those are release blockers to correct and review again.
Reviewers can disagree about taste even above 90.
The report should preserve that disagreement rather than promise universal enthusiasm.

## Konomi

Canon makes her a brisk, sharp-looking kitsune diplomat who uses courtesy as pressure and serves Nerosyan's political interests.
She can be condescending and manipulative, yet openly fearless when the Commander challenges her.
The desired romantic arc should preserve that competence.
Do not reveal that her whole political identity was a mask awaiting the right lover.

Begin with a private correction to the minutes after a hostile council meeting.
She has removed an insult to the Commander while leaving her disagreement intact.
A second meeting concerns a guest list, where mutual attraction first becomes an inconvenience she cannot file away.
Their first private invitation should be exact enough to sound like Konomi and personal enough to expose her.
A later leaked letter gives both sides an actual cost.
She might defend the relationship publicly while refusing a proposed alliance, or keep it discreet while accepting personal responsibility for the leak.
A political disagreement must remain possible on every romantic branch.

Her established chemistry comes from precision meeting resistance.
A useful original voice sample is: "The council requires my answer by morning. You may have mine now, if you can stop arguing long enough to ask."
Avoid ubiquitous tail flicks and fox jokes.
A recognizable kitsune likeness and formal clothing can loosen privately without converting her into a generic human court lady.

Konomi can introduce Arsinoe through the practical task of arranging a reception.
That should open an independent invitation, never trade the cleric's attention as a political favor.

## Jerribeth

The installed text identifies an oolioddroo demon using the guise of an elf sorceress.
Her true voice buzzes and chitters, her hands have delicate claws, and her interests include preserving Xanthir's captured swarm.
Her treatment of Wintersun and Marhevok is amusement and control, not an ordinary unhappy marriage.
Use the existing rivalry with Xanthir and her willingness to betray Baphomet as the opening for a new alliance.

The first romantic turn should occur when the Commander recognizes an illusion and chooses to continue the conversation anyway.
She first interprets this as arrogance or another performance.
Later, she tries a familiar manipulation, receives a refusal, and must decide whether a freely given return visit interests her enough to change that specific behavior.
Do not make one tender night reform her whole morality.
An evil alliance and a narrower negotiated attachment can remain separate outcomes.
Neither should require magical control over the Commander's desire.

Marhevok belongs in the consequences of the deception, not in a male-inclusive romantic scene.
If he survives, the story must acknowledge the collapse or continuation of his attachment according to the actual quest outcome.
Gesmerha must never be offered as Jerribeth's gift.
A joint scene should concern accountability or competing claims about Wintersun, not assume a romance between them.

Art requires both an explicitly labeled elf illusion and recognizable insect features in her revealed form.
Replacing her with a horned succubus fails fidelity regardless of image quality.

## Kiana

Kiana worships Desna, stages a playful vampire wedding, enjoys dresses and red wine, and uses humor to manage fear.
Her eyes are described as striking blue-green.
She directly complains that the demonic attack prevented her wedding night, embarrassing Elan.
She is already frank and playful, not a meek bride waiting to become interesting.

Start with an invitation to help choose a story or performance for a gathering before the wedding.
Attraction grows through shared theatrical play and small private exchanges.
The wedding attack must then interrupt the route honestly.
If her soul is absent, new romantic meetings stop until she returns.
A rescue cannot itself count as agreement to romance.

After recovery, her concern is whether the life everyone congratulates her on is the life she actually wants.
One branch permits an affair followed by an actual separation conversation.
Another allows her to confront the attraction before betraying her vows, then separate or remain married without a continued romance.
An Elan death branch must be written as bereavement with time for her to regain ordinary desires, not a shortcut that awards his widow.
Do not invent cruelty in Elan to make the player blameless.

Seelah can introduce her and later confront the Commander about the hurt caused to her friends.
Arsinoe can offer a quieter conversation after the recovery, with her own response to being the officiant whose ceremony ended in catastrophe.
These are excellent interconnections because they arise from existing relationships.
Neither woman must end her own romance just because another romance exists.

## Vellexia

Her gentleness in public sits beside extreme cruelty, magical domination, and a dangerous appetite for novelty.
Canon already presents dates and patronage.
The expansion must extend those encounters and their survival conditions rather than reset her as an unknown admirer.
Preserve her age, authority, and demonic identity.

Begin after an existing encounter with an invitation that assumes the Commander will perform for her.
The interesting reversal is a visit where the Commander refuses the entertainment but remains at the table.
She tries to provoke a reaction, then eventually reveals an opinion or memory without staging a spectacle around it.
Attachment frightens her because the next meeting begins to matter more than the next novelty.
A ruthless mutual fascination and a fragile attachment with actual limits should produce different later scenes.
Neither should erase the people she has harmed.

Wenduag already reacts jealously to Vellexia in the base text.
With jealousy restrictions disabled, turn the new scene toward rank and whether Vellexia is treating Wenduag as disposable.
Wenduag can secure an invitation to prove she understands the city's predators, then be surprised by her own concern for the Commander.
She cannot give Vellexia consent on the Commander's behalf or secure another woman as property.
An original Wenduag line for that situation: "I found you an invitation. You can survive one dinner without letting her put a collar on your pride."

## Seelah

The wedding speech places friendship, pleasure, and love inside her opposition to the Abyss.
She should remain physically confident, funny, capable of embarrassment, and morally serious without becoming a stern Irabeth duplicate.
Give her shared activities before confessions.
A contest, dancing after a poor performance, repairing a mistake for a friend, or finding a meal after a long watch suits her better than another seated discussion of boundaries.

Her first kiss can be uncomplicated pleasure.
The complication comes later when the Commander disappoints someone she cares about.
She should make a concrete demand connected to that harm, such as telling Kiana the truth before a celebration, rather than reciting an abstract code.
On an evil path she can remain attracted while contesting actions and choosing how to remain present.
Continued romance must not require her to endorse atrocities or abandon her faith.
All-path availability needs authored circumstances for separation in place, correspondence, or changed meetings where the main game's party state removes her.
The current chapter-only dialogue engine cannot imply she is physically present after departure.

## Arsinoe

She is an Abadar cleric from Absalom with a dusky face and molten-gold eyes.
She values organized civic life, officiates marriages, treats the victims of the stolen souls, and can show visible emotion in ceremony.
Her romance should concern the possibility of creating a durable home while a city keeps coming apart around her.
Do not reduce her to accounting puns or a generic severe priestess.

Begin with a walk after a civic task, where she remembers which household will need help tomorrow.
The Commander discovers a precise but amused woman outside ceremonial speech.
A later date can involve a public festival she helped arrange and has forgotten to enjoy.
Her difficult scene concerns a promise the Commander made casually that other people relied upon.
A private commitment and a public commitment offer different endings without making monogamy a requirement.
Use Kiana and Seelah's quest to establish professional and emotional connections, but keep patient care separate from romantic persuasion.

## Gesmerha

She is a blind woodshaper whose eyes Marhevok took, and she may become the chief who helps Wintersun endure or leads its departure.
Her craft treats carving as revealing beauty in a new form.
Her growing firmness after liberation is a better foundation for romance than helplessness.

Begin with the Commander handling an unfinished carving under her direction.
Let her correct clumsy assistance and make an ordinary joke.
Attraction should develop while she is making something for herself, not while the Commander dispenses aid to dependent villagers.
Later she must choose where her people will live.
The romance can survive either decision through visits and correspondence.
A final scene involving a finished object should pay off an early detail without declaring that the Commander has healed her whole life.

Do not restore her eyesight as the attractive ending.
Keep the blind appearance in art, avoid visual narration that gives her sight, and give her touch or sound without supernatural compensatory omniscience.
She and Jerribeth are connected by injury and political consequences.
They are not an established female couple and should not become a ready-made throuple.

## Minagho and Chivarro

Canon explicitly describes their progression from violent rivalry through obsession to partnership.
Chivarro's concern after hearing Minagho was spared is personal and vulnerable.
The existing RanRomance ending already accommodates both women with the Commander.
Expand that shared relationship rather than treating Chivarro as an unrelated acquisition.

Give each woman a private scene before a shared invitation.
Chivarro organizes space and favors, while Minagho hides care behind schemes and irritation.
The central conflict can be whether the couple's old habit of plotting against each other survives the addition of someone who refuses to be recruited as a secret ally.
Neither woman needs to become harmless.
They do need a reason to choose the arrangement again after the first successful night.
A later three-person disagreement should permit each woman a distinct desire and a resolution that remembers which promise the Commander actually made.
Preserve both lilitu identities and eyeless faces in art.

## All-route and multi-romance requirements for the writer

`Rules.Available` currently blocks scenes on `swarm`, `true_lich`, and either Tirabade death or departure, and excludes chapter 4 outside Memory scenes.
Those global rules are incompatible with adding independent romances and with the requested all-path availability.
Eligibility needs each scene's actual participants and location or communication mode.
Romantic availability across mythic paths does not by itself establish physical presence after a death, departure, hostile encounter, or loss of a soul.
Write and test those states rather than silently ignoring them.
The expanded route must not sacrifice another lover or clear another mod's romance state.
Jealousy-free compatibility should remove exclusivity enforcement while preserving reactions to lying, humiliation, and broken specific promises.
Those are different conflicts and can be written independently.

The wording of new shared scenes should support the requested female-only partner grouping.
Kiana's husband and Marhevok may exist in background consequences without becoming sexual or romantic participants with the Commander.
Do not infer a new female couple solely because two women have met.
Wenduag can introduce, scheme, compete, and misjudge.
Each introduced adult woman must still choose whether to engage.

## Trickster fate interventions

The user's added direction prioritizes Trickster and permits an authored reopening of missed, fatal, or hostile quest outcomes.
This can provide access without rewriting anyone's affection.
Track a restored encounter separately from interest and commitment.
A woman restored by a cosmic joke should remember enough to have a reaction to it, and that reaction need not be gratitude.
The original quest result must not continue to produce contradictory death narration beside the reopened route.
If the implementation leaves vanilla history intact, frame the restored woman explicitly as someone brought forward from a divergent possibility who knows what happened in this timeline.
That requires a stable explanation and dialogue after restoration, not a resurrection button with no story.

For Konomi, fate can return a letter to sender so thoroughly that the departing diplomat finds herself back at the table where she signed it.
She immediately asks who has authority to tamper with diplomatic correspondence, then notices that the seal bears her own signature.
She gets a new reason to negotiate a return, and her answer remains hers.

For Jerribeth, reverse the collector's predicament.
The Commander finds an impossible specimen label bearing her name and releases the possibility it has pinned in place.
She recognizes the indignity of being catalogued and wants to know who is keeping the collection.
This can lead to a new alliance or personal meeting even if her earlier encounter ended fatally.
Do not write resurrection as repayment in kisses.

For Kiana, restore the unwritten last page of the wedding play after the soul quest has reached its selected outcome.
A surviving Kiana can stage the performance she never finished, while a fate-restored Kiana knows this invitation has arrived after an ending that should have excluded her.
Keep her wedding, separation, and bereavement states distinct.
Restoring her must not also decide whether she leaves Elan.

For Vellexia, a portrait refuses to portray a dead or permanently absent hostess.
It invites her to complain about the likeness in person.
She is furious that a work of art has presumed to survive her and fascinated by the Commander's part in the insult.
The restored conversation begins with a contest of taste and authority, leaving attraction to develop afterward.

For Seelah, a missing line in a campfire story becomes a road back to the fire.
She realizes what happened and insists on learning what the Commander has done since they parted.
A second chance to meet cannot erase the moral disagreement that caused her departure.
Her next choice can be renewed travel, visits, or correspondence, with the romance continuing through the mode she selects.

For Arsinoe, an unpaid debt in the city's ledger belongs to tomorrow rather than yesterday.
Correcting the date restores the cleric who was meant to witness its settlement.
She treats the miracle as a civic problem before permitting herself to react personally.
The later invitation comes after she chooses to stay, outside that debt.

For Gesmerha, an unfinished carving retains the sound of its maker's tools.
The Commander persuades the story that the maker has stepped outside for a moment and brings that possibility back.
She identifies the carving by touch and objects to the changes someone made while she was gone.
Keep blindness and craft intact.
A restored Wintersun chief still has people whose fate matters to her.

For Minagho and Chivarro, a threat never delivered becomes an invitation delivered twice.
Each assumes the other forged it and arrives prepared for betrayal.
The Commander must let them recognize each other and settle the old argument before proposing a place in their life.
Restore missing participants into the existing RanRomance progression rather than creating parallel flags that can yield two contradictory endings.

These interventions are distinct entry scenes, not substitutes for full routes.
Other mythic paths need a setting-appropriate way to access the same renewed encounter, while Trickster receives these specific comic and unsettling versions.
The relationship still needs its own attraction, conflict, private pleasure, and continuing choice afterward.

## Seelah opening review, first delivered draft

Reviewed `storylines/seelah.py` containing `seelah.boots`, `seelah.wager`, and `seelah.promise`.
This score concerns these three opening scenes only.
It does not approve a complete romance, mature later development, all-path integration, or installed gameplay.
Provisional score: 82/100.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |

| Character voice and canon fidelity | 25/30 | Generous, playful, practical Seelah is recognizable, but some ornate wit and habitual coyness flatten her confident directness |
| Adult emotional complexity | 17/20 | The double-booked promise is a believable disagreement with three defensible responses |
| Romantic tension | 13/15 | The dancing and purposeful closeness work, though physical confidence repeatedly retreats into the same shy gesture |
| Branching and consequences | 15/20 | Activity and preference branches exist, but common text ignores some answers and the rescheduled day is only narrated |

### What works

`boots/start` establishes warmth before demanding vulnerability.
The sock joke suits a paladin who enjoys ordinary life and refuses needless solemnity.
`wager/walk` lets her helpfulness interrupt her own romantic plans, showing a trait through activity.
`promise/start` builds on that trait instead of inventing a new crisis.
The player can help, ask her to delegate, or ask her to make a new plan.
None is simply the cruel answer, and `promise/keep` permits lingering discomfort after a reasonable decision.
Keep that branch.
`promise/kiss` gives her initiative and connects the kiss to an earlier shared joke.
The intimacy is mild, which suits an opening and is not a defect by itself.

### Continuity and choice responses to fix

`boots/meal` says Seelah cuts the apple into slices.
`boots/home` then has her turning the apple to find the next cut, while `boots/plans` removes a blemish and hands over the remaining half.
`boots/rest` also lets the Commander take the apple before she cuts it.
Choose one consistent sequence, for example let her cut as she listens and use slices in every branch afterward.
The final choice in `boots/finish` should not leave her a last slice after a branch has already said the Commander ate the remaining half.

`wager/ask` offers "Show me this dance you were planning" before Seelah has mentioned dancing on that path.
Have her suggest a dance in `ask`, or make the player's choice an original invitation such as "Yes. Do you dance?"
`wager/dance_end` begins "And miss your face when I called it a contest of skill?" after either a spoken question or a silent move closer.
That answer only responds to the question.
Give the silent choice its own short response, or use a common opening that addresses the movement before recalling the wager.

`promise/kiss` closes a hand against a blanket and `promise/worry` picks a thread from it.
Only `promise/later` introduced a blanket.
Introduce the blanket in the common `promise/supper` setup, or remove it from the common nodes.
`promise/later` promises tomorrow and jumps directly to the following evening within the same scene.
This bypasses the exact delay the choice is about.
Split the return into a follow-up scene with a delay and the `seelah.rescheduled` requirement, or stage the whole event explicitly as a book-event time passage and actually account for time.
Do not narrate a meaningful appointment that the game cannot remember as an appointment.

`wager/start` establishes a game, but every branch abandons it without either player throwing a button or anyone acknowledging the abandoned contest.
One throw before the invitation would pay off the premise and make the title accurate.
Alternatively, let her openly sweep the buttons away when the Commander accepts the invitation.
The button recovery callback can stay.

### Voice and prose revisions

`boots/start` says the floor has been following the Commander around asking for orders.
That image requires the reader to stop and solve the joke.
A direct observation about exhausted people still bringing requests would sound more like Seelah and establish the scene faster.
Her calling the button contest a matter of "extraordinary skill" works as a single mock-formal flourish.
But "the height of my splendor," "complain beautifully enough to distract them," and "considerably better than the buttons" distribute that same polished register across the whole opening.
Keep one flourish and let the rest become ordinary speech.
For example, "That went better than the buttons" is sufficient.
Use contractions naturally in her dialogue.

`boots/company` asks whether they enjoy each other "without an emergency," echoing the existing Irabeth opening's central phrasing.
The overlap in situation is reasonable, but copying the emotional framing makes Seelah feel closer to Irabeth than she should.
Let the Commander name something particular about Seelah, such as wanting to hear how a story ended, or admit attraction more plainly.

`boots/road`, `wager/ask`, and `promise/supper` repeatedly discover that she has become shy or surprised by her own interest.
One such turn is charming.
Let her be fully aware of flirting in another scene, and let the Commander be the one caught off guard.
Her confidence at the second kiss already shows how well that works.

`wager/terms` explains that she smiles as though caught doing something she meant to keep doing.
`wager/close` explains that her hand remains longer than the explanation requires.
`wager/walk_end` interprets a finger brush as a question.
These are three versions of the narrator explaining attraction immediately after a gesture shows it.
Keep the strongest and cut or replace the others with an actual response.
The same applies to `wager/dance_end` explaining that taking her hand now has no lesson attached.
Readers can infer it from the second invitation.

`promise/supper` summarizes a horse, a tune, and a well-fitting shirt, then `promise/time` claims the horse story makes both people laugh without giving them the joke.
Write one actual odd detail or short anecdote instead of a list of three whimsical placeholders.
The comedy should belong to Seelah's telling, not to a narrator informing the player that the evening was funny.

### Gameplay staging and consequences

All three scenes advertise availability across chapters 1 through 5, but assume a fire, guards, supplies, nearby musicians, and a setting that can be walked around safely.
The Abyss and hostile adventure maps need an explicit off-duty framing or a rest/location requirement.
A short book-event introduction such as a safe evening at camp can resolve much of this if the engine really places the encounter there.
Scene prose should not imply a physical setup absent from the gameplay state.

`boots/start` and `boots/rest` joke about orders and governing when the scene can occur before the player is Commander.
Use a chapter-aware line or a neutral joke about everyone needing help.
The metadata claims personal quests matter, but these scenes do not yet react to them.
That is acceptable for an opening, provided the eventual implementation fulfills the promise and the present review does not award those later consequences points.

The preference flags are useful, but future payoffs are not yet evidence.
Within the delivered opening, `seelah.day_home`, `seelah.day_road`, `seelah.quiet`, and `seelah.direct` have no visible later response.
Use one or two relevant callbacks in the next meeting rather than adding more flags.
For example, she can remember that the Commander wanted quiet and propose the walk directly, or make a different joke about the home they imagined.
Reviewers should inspect the actual text selected by those states.

### Conditions for a stronger resubmission

Correct the apple, blanket, unmentioned dance, and mismatched reply first.
Then make the postponed appointment a remembered gameplay event, establish valid camp staging, and pay off one earlier preference in a visible line.
Reduce the repeated shy beats and explanatory attraction narration.
Give Seelah one fully voiced little story or joke in place of the supper summary.
These changes could move the delivered opening above 90, but the revised text must be reread and scored independently.
No promised score applies to an unseen revision.

### Remaining substantive revisions

The wager still has no resolution or acknowledged abandonment.
In `wager/start`, Seelah throws once and offers buttons, but the Commander never plays and she proceeds to a dance or walk without noticing.
A single common sentence at the start of each activity can fix this.
For example, before the dance she can pocket the buttons and say, "We'll call it a draw. Come on."
Before the walk, she can declare that both have won and leave the cup where it is.
Choose one natural line rather than add an unnecessary contest system.

The common `supper` node still lists a horse, a tune, and a well-fitting shirt instead of letting Seelah tell anything.
The `time` node then tells the player they laugh at a story the player never hears.
Give her an actual short anecdote with a concrete comic ending.
The horse can be afraid of the bucket until someone fills it, then follow the same bucket around camp demanding more.
She can complain that the horse now has a better supply officer than she does.
That would be ordinary Seelah humor with an observed absurdity, and the return to it after postponing the kiss would have a real referent.
The exact joke is an authoring choice, but a report of laughter does not replace a joke.

The same overly finished phrases flagged in the first review remain in `boots/company`, `wager/start`, and `kiss`.
"Height of my splendor" is defensible as a single mock-grand line.
"Loser tries not to complain beautifully enough to distract them" is strained and muddies the simple invitation.
"Loser carries the food" or "Loser isn't allowed to call it cheating" would be immediately legible.
"That went considerably better than the buttons" only needs "That went better than the buttons."
Seelah can be witty without every joke sounding written for quotation.

The emotional movement still repeats across `boots/company`, `boots/road`, `wager/ask`, and `supper`.
Each lets her begin confidently and then become shy, surprised, or unable to finish a sentence.
Let `wager/ask` be a fully deliberate invitation.
She has already spent an entire scene making room for the Commander.
Removing the explanation that directness makes her shy would preserve the charming admission that the buttons were an excuse, while giving the courtship a step forward.
Keep the more vulnerable pause before the first kiss, where it has greater weight.

The explanatory narration remains in `wager/terms`, `wager/close`, `wager/walk_end`, and `wager/dance_end`.
The last line about the hand having "no lesson attached" can simply go.
The request for another dance has already made that point.
The finger brush and the two player choices already communicate an invitation without calling it a question.
Cutting those explanations improves confidence in the prose and leaves more room for Seelah's own voice.
